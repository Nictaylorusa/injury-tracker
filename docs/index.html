What these numbers are

Stays and games missed are built from the NBA's official injury reports, which teams file before every game, using the public archive in the sportsdataverse project (the hoopR "nba_stats_injury_report" release). For each game and player I take the last version of the report. A game counts as missed when the player is listed Out for an injury, illness, injury-related rest ("Rest – knee management") or return-to-competition reconditioning. G League assignments, personal reasons, suspensions, "not with team" and plain rest are not counted. A stay is a run of consecutive team games listed Out; if a player is listed Questionable or Probable in between, it starts a new stay. Regular season, play-in and playoff games are included. Preseason and summer league are not.

Checks: Haliburton in 2025-26 comes to 82 games and Lillard to 88, matching HoopsMatic's counts. Full-season league totals run about 3–5% above HoopsMatic's, which counts only confirmed roster players.

Limits: "listed Out" is what the team filed, not a medical diagnosis. Games a player missed without being listed are not here. Right now shows each team's report for its next game; the archive can run up to a day behind the NBA's own site.

References

The "Typical recovery" ranges are approximate return-to-play ranges for each kind of injury, matched automatically from the NBA's wording. They are general reference ranges, not a medical opinion and not a prediction for any one player. The team's medical staff should review them before anyone relies on them.

Numbers checked against published studies

  • Hamstring: Reurink G, Whiteley R, Tol JL. Return to play after acute hamstring injuries. Aspetar Sports Medicine Journal, 2015. Reports mean times of 18, 24 and 60 days for MRI grades I, II and III in professional footballers, and a mean of 44 days in a cohort of 90 professional athletes.
  • ACL reconstruction: D'Ambrosi R, et al. The majority of elite and professional athletes return to the preinjury level of activity after ACL reconstruction. Knee Surgery, Sports Traumatology, Arthroscopy, 2025. Pooled mean return of 292 days (95% CI 268 to 316).

General references used to sense-check the rest

  • Bass Medical Group, Common NBA Injuries and Recovery Times (ankle sprain grades, Achilles rupture, hamstring, MCL, meniscus, labrum, fractures).
  • WTOP, When can I return to play after an orthopedic sports injury? (2018; collarbone, calf strain, ankle sprain, finger fracture).

These two pages describe ranges in words, not exact numbers. Every other range in the table is a general estimate made for this tracker, and hovering over a range shows which kind it is. The full list of ranges is in the file recovery_timelines.json, which can be edited.

N.i.C.E. Scouting

The "N.i.C.E. Scouting" column lists offensive actions that a defender with each kind of injury may have trouble guarding, such as stop-and-go dribbles against patellar tendon problems or pump fakes and putbacks against Achilles and soleus problems. When the injury names a side, the first item says which way to attack (against a right knee or ankle, drive to his left or cross back toward his right so he has to plant on that leg). Each item is the movements that load that kind of injury, turned around into how to attack it. That is general reasoning by this tracker for scouting, not a medical statement or a prediction for any one player, and it assumes he is playing through it. Three of the injury groups rest on the published reviews below for the underlying movements; the rest are general sports-science reasoning. Hovering over a cell shows which.

  • Patellar tendon: Van der Worp H, et al. Jumper's knee or lander's knee? A systematic review of the relation between jump biomechanics and patellar tendinopathy. International Journal of Sports Medicine, 2014. Concludes that horizontal landing after forward acceleration carries the greatest risk, and that stiff landing patterns are linked to onset.
  • Hamstring: Late swing or early stance? A narrative review of hamstring injury mechanisms during high-speed running. Scandinavian Journal of Medicine and Science in Sports, 2019. Concludes that late swing is the most likely injury phase, though direct evidence is limited.
  • Achilles: Habets B, et al. Return to sport in athletes with midportion Achilles tendinopathy. Sports Medicine, 2018. Describes running and jumping as the activities resumed once symptoms settle. It does not cover calf or soleus injuries specifically.

Pace is the middle of the range minus days out so far, counted from the first missed game. Players whose injury is described only as "injury management" or "injury recovery" show a dash, because those words don't say what the injury is.
